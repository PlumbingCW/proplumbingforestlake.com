Sump pump failure: what to do right now

  • Check that the pump is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped.
  • Look at the float. If it is stuck against the pit wall, free it.
  • If you have a bucket and the water is shallow, bail into a utility sink or outside, away from the foundation.
  • Never touch the pump or cord while standing in water. Turn off the breaker for the area first if you can do so safely.

Most failures come from a stuck float, a clogged intake, a dead motor, or a power outage during a storm. Storms are exactly when a pump matters and when the power is most likely to fail, which is why a battery backup is worth considering.

Where the water is allowed to go in Forest Lake

The City of Forest Lake's code of ordinances, in Chapter 52, prohibits roof, sump pump, and footing tile water from entering the sanitary sewer. Sump and footing lines must be permanent, year-round, and run outside the building or to a city storm sewer. The city may inspect, and properties found noncompliant can be charged a monthly fee. The online code notes it may not reflect the most recent changes, so confirm with the city.

The code also allows an exception. From November 15 to March 15, the City Administrator may permit sump discharge to the sanitary sewer. That can apply if freezing creates a dangerous condition, the pipes are at risk of freezing, or the water cannot reach a storm drain. The permit requires an inspection in March. Ask the city before connecting anything.

Installing or replacing a pump

Check the pit

The pit should be deep enough, lined, and have a lid. A pit with debris or a cracked liner gets repaired.

Choose the pump

A submersible pump is the common choice. Size depends on how much water comes in and how high it must be lifted.

Set the pump and check valve

A check valve keeps water from flowing back. The float is set so it moves freely.

Route the discharge

The line leads outside, away from the foundation, and not to the sanitary sewer.

Dual-pump systems and battery backups add protection. A water-powered or battery pump keeps working when the power is out. Sump discharge pipe repairs and drainage are also part of the work, and chronic water entry can point at the need for basement waterproofing.

Ejector pumps versus sump pumps

A sump pump moves groundwater to the outside. An ejector pump moves wastewater from a basement bathroom up to the sewer line. They look similar but have different covers, vents, and rules. Mixing them up is a common source of code violations, so the plumber confirms which one you have before work starts. Basement bathroom plumbing is covered under plumbing installation.
