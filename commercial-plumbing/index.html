When commercial plumbing cannot wait

A burst or leaking supply line

Water on the floor or through a ceiling can damage stock, equipment, and neighbors in a shared building.

Backing up floor drains

A restaurant or shop with sewage in the floor drain usually has to close until the line is cleared.

No hot water

A failed water heater can close a salon, kitchen, or medical office.

Restrooms out of service

Overflowing toilets or no water can be a code and customer problem at the same time.

Commercial buildings differ from houses in scale. Pipes are larger, fixtures are many, and a failure can affect several tenants. The plumber works out the building's layout and isolates the problem without shutting off more than necessary.

Grease and drain lines in commercial kitchens

Cooking grease cools in drain lines and builds up. Restaurants with a grease trap or interceptor need it cleaned on a schedule, and the lines leading to it benefit from periodic hydro jetting. Waiting until the line backs up usually means a messy emergency in the middle of service.

A regular maintenance plan for drain lines, traps, and floor drains is cheaper than emergency calls. The plumber can set a schedule that fits the business's volume and hours.

Backflow testing and code items

Many commercial buildings have backflow prevention devices that protect the public water supply from contamination. These devices need periodic testing, and the water utility or code official may require documentation. Ask the Forest Lake building department and your water utility what applies to your building.

Other planned work includes fixture replacement, water heater replacement with the right capacity, and sewer camera inspections for older buildings. A look at the main line with a camera inspection is a useful baseline when taking over a property.

Safety steps before the plumber arrives

  • Close the main or the affected branch valve and tell your staff where it is.
  • Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and standing water.
  • Switch off power to wet equipment only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurance claim before cleanup starts.
  • Stop using restrooms and floor drains if sewage is coming up.

What the city asks of residents

According to the City of Forest Lake, wipes, feminine hygiene products, paper towels, floss, and cat litter do not belong in the sewer. When a backup is reported, the city checks the public main first, and the pipe from the main to your house is the owner's to maintain.
