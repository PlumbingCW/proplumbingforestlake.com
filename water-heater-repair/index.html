Leak or no hot water: what to check first

Most water heater calls start with one of two problems. The tank is wet, or the water is cold. They are very different jobs, and the first answer is whether the water is coming from the tank itself or from a pipe, valve, or fitting on top of it.

Common water heater symptoms and likely causes
What you seeLikely causeRepair or replace

A leaking tank usually cannot be patched

If water is seeping through the tank shell, the steel liner has failed. No sealant fixes that for long, and a tank that has started to leak can fail suddenly. The honest advice is to shut off the cold supply, drain it, and move to water heater replacement.

A leak at the top is different. Loose supply connections, a weeping temperature and pressure relief valve, or a bad drain valve can all drip, and those are real repairs. A plumber confirms which one it is before recommending anything.

Gas, electric, and hybrid units fail in different ways

A gas unit depends on a clean burner, a working thermocouple, and a clear flue. A pilot that will not stay lit, soot at the draft hood, or a flame that lifts or turns yellow are safety issues as well as heating issues.

An electric tank has two elements and two thermostats. When the upper one fails, you lose hot water quickly. When the lower one fails, you get hot water that runs out fast. A hybrid heat pump unit adds a compressor and fan, plus a condensate line that can clog.

When repair makes sense and when it does not

Repair usually makes sense when

  • The tank is under about 10 years old and not leaking
  • The failed part is an element, thermostat, thermocouple, or valve
  • The unit heats fine and only one thing is acting up

Replacement usually makes sense when

  • The tank shell is leaking
  • The unit is well past 10 to 12 years old
  • Repairs cost a large share of a new unit

A tank with a few years left deserves a repair. One that is old, rusting, and already on its second part is a poor place to keep spending. The plumber lays out both costs so you can decide, and a tankless switch is covered under tankless water heater service.

Forest Lake city water and your water heater

City water in Forest Lake is softened at the treatment plants and averages 5.5 to 6.5 grains per gallon, which the city places in the moderately hard range. That is gentler on a tank than untreated hard water, but sediment and the anode rod still wear over time, so an older tank can still fail.

If your home is on a private well in a rural part of the area, the water chemistry can be very different. Hardness, iron, or acidity can shorten a tank's life, and a water test tells you what you are dealing with.

Safety steps before the plumber arrives

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911.
  • Turn off the power or set the gas control to pilot before you touch the unit.
  • Close the cold water inlet valve if the tank is leaking.
  • Keep away from the relief valve discharge, because the water can scald.

Checking a plumber's license

According to the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry, plumbing contractors must hold a state license, with a responsible master plumber and a surety bond. The license lookup at dli.mn.gov lets you confirm any plumber before you hire.
