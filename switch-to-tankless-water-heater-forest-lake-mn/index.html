Should I Switch to a Tankless Water Heater? Forest Lake, MN Guide

A tankless water heater gives hot water on demand and takes up little space, but it is not a drop-in swap. It asks for a bigger gas supply, different venting, and an annual flush, and in a cold climate it has to work harder.

This guide lays out the decision for Forest Lake homeowners so you can tell whether the switch will pay off in your house.

The upfront cost gap is the main hurdle. The savings come from lower standby heat loss and, in some homes, a longer service life. Whether that pays off depends on how much hot water you use and how much the conversion costs.

Cold inlet water in Minnesota

A tankless heater raises the temperature of incoming water by a set amount. In winter, groundwater is colder, so the unit has to add more heat, and the flow it can deliver at a given set point drops. A unit sized for summer can disappoint in January.

Ask the plumber to check the unit's rated flow at a winter temperature rise, not just the headline number. If you run two showers and a dishwasher at once, the capacity matters.

A decision checklist

  • Your gas line can carry the unit, or you are willing to upgrade it.
  • There is a good route for the new venting.
  • You use enough hot water to value an endless supply.
  • You will flush the unit every year.
  • The upfront cost fits your budget.

If most of those are yes, tankless is worth a serious look, and the tankless water heater page covers installation and flushing. If not, a new tank through water heater installation may be the better choice.

Sizing a tankless unit for your household

Tankless units are rated by flow in gallons per minute at a given temperature rise. List your fixtures and how many run together. A shower may use around two gallons a minute, a dishwasher a bit more, and a washing machine similar. Add the ones that run at the same time to find your peak demand.

Then check the unit's output at the temperature rise you need in winter. A higher rise lowers the flow. If your peak is close to the unit's limit, size up, because a unit running at its ceiling wears faster and gives lukewarm water.

Recirculation and wait time

A tankless heater does not remove the wait for hot water to reach a distant fixture. A recirculation loop or a small buffer tank can shorten it. Ask the plumber whether the unit supports recirculation and what it adds.

Hard water and tankless maintenance

Mineral scale builds up inside a tankless heat exchanger and cuts flow over time. Forest Lake city water is softened to an average of about 5.5 to 6.5 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard, so scale is slower than on untreated water but still real.

Most makers call for a periodic flush with vinegar or a descaling solution, and a plumber can set up service valves to make that easy. Skipping it shortens the life of the unit and is the most common reason for lukewarm output a few years in.

Gas line and vent changes

Many tankless units need a larger gas line than a tank heater, and most need a sealed vent made of a specific material. Ask the plumber whether the existing line and vent can be reused, and have the answer in the written quote.

Checking a plumber's license

According to the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry, plumbing contractors must hold a state license, with a responsible master plumber and a surety bond. The license lookup at dli.mn.gov lets you confirm any plumber before you hire.

Forest Lake water and tankless care

The City of Forest Lake softens its water at the treatment plants to an average of 5.5 to 6.5 grains per gallon. That reduces scale on a tankless heat exchanger compared with raw hard water, but the maker's flush schedule still applies. Ask your plumber to install isolation valves so the flush is easy.

Homes on private wells, as in Columbus and Scandia, may have harder or iron-rich water that fouls a tankless unit faster. Test the water before you buy.
