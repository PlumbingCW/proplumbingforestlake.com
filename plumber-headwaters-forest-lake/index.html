Where Headwaters is

Local reporting describes Headwaters as a planned neighborhood of about 620 acres west of U.S. 61, just south of the Forest Lake airport. It was designed mainly for single-family homes, with room for multifamily housing, senior housing and retail. Headwaters Parkway, the street that carries the name, is also the northern end of a regional sewer project covered below.

What is documented about the homes

The developer began planning Headwaters in 2004 and expected to finish its phases by 2012. The recession delayed that, and the same reporting says only six homes had been built as of 2013 before another builder took over the work. Homes in Headwaters were therefore built over a long stretch of years, and most are likely newer than housing in the older parts of Forest Lake. That reporting dates from 2018, so the current build-out is not confirmed here.

Verified, general and unknown

Plumbing advice for a neighborhood works best when it separates what is documented from what is assumed.

  • Verified: the size and location of Headwaters, the 2004 start of planning, the phased build, and the regional sewer project at Headwaters Parkway.
  • General: any home has a main shutoff, a water heater with a limited life, and drains that can clog. These points apply anywhere.
  • Unknown: the year any single home was built, its pipe materials, and whether a given address is on city water and sewer. Your utility bill and the county property record answer those.

A regional sewer project at Headwaters Parkway

According to the Metropolitan Council, its Forest Lake Sewer Improvements project rehabilitates an aging sewer interceptor that runs from Headwaters Parkway to 180th Street North, with lift station work in Forest Lake and Hugo. Design ran from 2023 through 2025, and the April 2026 update lists construction for summer 2026 with final restoration in spring 2027.

An interceptor is a large regional pipe. It is separate from the lateral that joins your house to the city main, and the city has said the owner maintains that lateral. The project page does not describe any effect on individual homes, so questions about it belong with the Metropolitan Council or the City. Neighbors to the south can read about Hugo, which shares part of the project.

Practical steps for any newer home

  • Find the main water shutoff and turn it once a year so it does not seize.
  • Read the date on your water heater label and note it, because the label is the reliable record of age.
  • Check that sump pump or footing drain water does not go to the sanitary sewer, because city code section 52.23 prohibits that, with a seasonal exception from November 15 to March 15 by permit.
  • Ask your builder or the county for records before assuming what material your pipes are.
  • If a builder warranty may still apply, read its terms before paying for a repair.
