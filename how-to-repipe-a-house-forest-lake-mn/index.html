How Do You Repipe a House? Forest Lake, MN Homeowner Guide

Repiping a house means replacing the water supply pipes, usually all of them, in a single project. It sounds like demolition, but a good plan keeps the wall openings small and the water off for only part of each day.

This guide explains the process step by step for Forest Lake homeowners, how to choose a pipe material, and what to ask before you hire.

Decide whether repiping is the answer

Repiping is for failing pipe, not a single leak. A string of leaks, rusty water, falling pressure, or a pipe material known to fail are the usual triggers. If you are not sure, leak detection can show whether the problem is local or spread through the system.

Step by step through a repipe

Inspect and plan

The plumber identifies the pipe material, counts fixtures, and maps routes through the basement, attic, and walls.

Set the scope and permit

You agree on the scope, including which fixtures and valves are replaced, and the permit is arranged.

Open access points

Small holes are cut in drywall where lines must pass, usually near fixtures and in closets.

Run new pipe

New lines are pulled or run to each fixture, tied to the main shutoff and the water heater.

Test and patch

The system is pressure tested, and holes are patched ready for paint.

PEX versus copper

Choosing a pipe material
QuestionPEXCopper
Speed of installationFaster, fewer fittingsSlower, more soldering
Freeze toleranceBetter, it can expand slightlyCan split when frozen
Track recordDecades of use, newer than copperVery long track record
Wall openingsSmaller, flexible pipeLarger, rigid pipe
Material costTypically lowerTypically higher

Both are code-approved for supply plumbing. PEX has become the common choice for whole-house repipes because it is quicker and needs fewer openings. Copper is still chosen for exposed runs and by owners who prefer it. Your plumber explains what fits your layout.

Living through a repipe

  • Expect the water off for several hours each day for a few days.
  • Plan for drywall dust and furniture moved from work areas.
  • Ask about a temporary water source for the day, such as a hose bib line.
  • Paint and wall finishing are usually a separate step from the plumbing.
  • Keep pets and children away from open walls and tools.

For a short repair after a single leak, burst pipe repair may be enough. For a full system, the repiping page describes the service in more detail.

Questions to ask a plumber before a repipe

  • What material do you recommend for my layout, and why?
  • How many access holes do you expect and where?
  • Will the water heater connections and shutoffs be replaced too?
  • Who handles the permit and inspection?
  • Is drywall patching included, and who paints?
  • What does the written warranty cover?

Get the answers in writing. A repipe is a large project, and vague scope is the main cause of disputes. If two quotes differ widely, compare what each includes before comparing the totals.

Signs a repipe went well

Water pressure should be even at every fixture, with no noises in the walls when you turn a tap on or off. The water should run clear after the first flush. Walk through the house with the plumber and open every faucet before they leave, because a missed fixture is easy to fix on the day and harder a month later.

Who operates the street valve

According to the City of Forest Lake Water Department, the curb stop at the street is operated by city staff only. Do not count on it to stop water in an emergency, and close the main valve inside your home instead.

Forest Lake and lead service lines

The City of Forest Lake publishes a lead service line inventory. Its 2023 inventory lists 408 lead services, 46 galvanized services requiring replacement, and 3,895 non-lead lines, with no unknowns. The city points residents to the Lead Inventory Tracking Tool at the University of Minnesota to look up an address.

A repipe replaces the pipes inside your house, and the city is responsible for the water main and the line to the curb stop, while the part beyond is the customer's. If your service line is lead or galvanized, plan the replacement together with the interior work so the ground is opened once. Homeowners in Hugo and Wyoming should ask their own city for a service line inventory.
