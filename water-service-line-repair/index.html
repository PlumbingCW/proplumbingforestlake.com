Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Forest Lake, MN

A leak in the water line between the street and your house can flood a yard or basement and run up a bill fast, and the 24/7 line is open. Shut off the main valve inside the house. Do not touch the curb stop at the street, because the city says only its employees may operate it.

The water service line is the underground pipe that carries water from the city main, through a curb stop and the meter, into your home. This page covers leaks, low pressure, no-water calls, and replacement of old lead and galvanized lines for Forest Lake homeowners.

Signs of a service line problem

Wet or soft spots in the yard

Water pooling between the street and the house with no rain is a leaking service line until proven otherwise.

A high water bill

The City of Forest Lake reads meters quarterly, so a slow underground leak can run for weeks before the bill shows it.

Low pressure at every fixture

A partly failed line or a corroded pipe can strangle flow.

Water meter that never stops

If it moves with every fixture off, water is escaping somewhere past the meter.

Who is responsible for which part?

The City of Forest Lake's Water Department says the city maintains the water main and the line up to the curb stop, and that the line beyond it is the customer's. To tell which side a leak is on, the city shuts off the curb stop. If water keeps flowing, the leak is on the city side. If it stops, the leak is on the customer side.

Only city employees may operate the curb stop. That is worth knowing before you call, because the first diagnostic step on a suspected service line leak often involves the city, and a plumber can help you coordinate.

Lead and galvanized service lines in Forest Lake

The city publishes a lead service line inventory on its Water Department page. The 2023 inventory lists 408 lead services and 46 galvanized services requiring replacement, with 3,895 non-lead lines, and the city points residents to the University of Minnesota Lead Inventory Tracking Tool to look up an address.

If your service line is lead or galvanized, a leak is a good moment to plan the full replacement, because opening the ground twice costs more. Ask the plumber what material your line is, and ask the city about its replacement plans for the public side.

How a service line is found and fixed

Locate the line

The plumber uses the meter, a locator, and the house entry point to trace the path.

Pinpoint the leak

Acoustic or pressure methods narrow the leak to a short stretch. See leak detection for how that works.

Excavate or pull a new line

A small pit for a spot repair, or two pits for trenchless replacement.

Test and restore

The line is pressure tested, the water turned back on, and the area backfilled.

Winter adds a problem. A line that is not buried deep enough or is exposed near the foundation can freeze. If the water stops at every tap on a very cold night, ask about a frozen service line, and for a pipe split inside the house see burst pipe repair.
