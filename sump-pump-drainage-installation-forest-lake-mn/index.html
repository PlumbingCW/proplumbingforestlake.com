Sump Pump Drainage Installation: Forest Lake, MN Homeowner Guide

A sump pump drainage system collects groundwater before it reaches your basement floor and sends it outside. It has four parts: a drain path, a pit, a pump, and a discharge line, and a weak link in any of them lets water through.

This guide explains how installation works for Forest Lake homeowners and what the city code adds to the plan.

The four parts of a drainage system

PartWhat it doesWhat to check
Drain tile or collection pathGathers water along the footingClear and pitched toward the pit

Pump and floatLifts water out of the pitSize, power, and a free-moving float

How a plumber installs one

Pick the location

The pit goes at the lowest point or where water enters, usually a corner near the wall.

Cut and dig the pit

A section of slab is cut, the soil is removed, and a liner is set to the proper depth.

Set the pump and check valve

The pump sits on a firm base with the float clear of the sides, and a check valve is added to the discharge.

Run the discharge

The line goes outside through the rim joist with a slope away from the house and an outlet that can drain.

Test with water

The pit is filled with water to confirm the pump starts, empties, and shuts off.

Add-ons worth considering

  • A battery backup pump for power outages.
  • A high-water alarm.
  • A second pump for large flows.
  • A sealed lid to reduce humidity and odor.
  • Interior drain tile to carry water to the pit from the whole perimeter.

If water enters from many points, a pit alone may not be enough, and basement waterproofing describes interior drain tile. Repairing an existing system is described under sump pump repair.

Sizing a pump to the water you actually get

Pump size is about volume and lift. A pump that is too small runs constantly and wears out. A pump that is too large short-cycles, turning on and off every few seconds. The right size depends on the rate water enters the pit and how high the discharge has to lift it.

Many homes do well with a third to half horsepower submersible pump, and homes with a high water table or a long discharge run may need more. A plumber estimates the flow by watching how fast the pit fills during wet weather.

Maintenance that keeps it working

  • Test the pump by pouring water into the pit every few months.
  • Clean the intake screen and remove silt from the pit.
  • Check the discharge outlet before winter and after storms.
  • Replace the pump before it reaches the end of its life.
  • Test the battery backup on a schedule if you have one.

Choosing where the water goes

The discharge point matters as much as the pump. Water should leave the pipe well away from the foundation and flow downhill, not back toward the house or onto a neighbor. In Forest Lake, city code does not allow sump water into the sanitary sewer, so the discharge has to go outside.

A pump that pushes water to a spot that floods back into the pit just cycles the same water again. Walk the yard during a rain and watch where it runs before you settle on the outlet.

What city code says about sump water

According to Forest Lake city code section 52.23, sump pump and footing drain water may not be discharged into the sanitary sewer. A seasonal exception from November 15 to March 15 exists by permit from the City Administrator, and noncompliance carries a monthly charge.

Forest Lake rules on sump water

The City of Forest Lake's code of ordinances, section 52.23, bars roof, sump pump, and footing tile water from the sanitary sewer. Sump and footing lines must be permanent, year-round, and run outside the building or to a city storm sewer. New dwellings with sumps permitted after January 1, 2004 had to have the pump piped to the outside before a certificate of occupancy.

Forest Lake also lies within the Comfort Lake-Forest Lake Watershed District, which reports that more than 8,160 acres of land and water drain into Forest Lake. For homeowners in Hugo and Wyoming, ask your own city for its discharge rules before routing a line.
