Emergency Hydro Jetting in Forest Lake, MN

When a main line is backing up and a snake only buys a few days, hydro jetting is often the next step, and our 24/7 line is open if it cannot wait. Stop using water, keep the floor drains clear of belongings, and do not pour chemicals in.

Hydro jetting is a method that sends high-pressure water through a nozzle inside the pipe to scour grease, scale, sludge, and small roots off the pipe wall. This page explains when it is the right tool for a Forest Lake home or business and when it is not.

Hydro jetting versus a snake

A cable machine drives a cutter through a clog and opens a path. It works well for a single blockage like a wad of paper or a root mass. What it leaves behind is the film of grease and scale along the pipe wall that sets up the next clog.

A jetter fires pressurized water from nozzles that point forward and backward, which pulls the hose along while scouring the pipe. The result is a pipe wall cleaned all the way around, not just a hole through the middle.

Why a camera check comes first

Water at high pressure can find weak spots. A cracked, offset, or collapsed pipe is not a candidate for jetting, because the blast can worsen the damage or flush debris into the break. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe condition before anything is fired through it.

If the camera shows a belly, a root intrusion at a joint, or a break, the plan changes. Roots at a joint can be cut and jetted, but a break needs sewer line repair. The plumber explains what the picture shows before recommending a step.

Where jetting does the most good

Kitchen drain lines

Cooking grease cools and coats the pipe, and a line to the main can narrow year after year.

Main lines with repeat backups

A line that backs up every few months usually has buildup, not just one clog.

Restaurant and commercial lines

Grease and food waste build up quickly. Jetting is a standard maintenance step.

Slow drains throughout the house

Several slow fixtures with no single clog often means the main is narrowed by scale.

What the City of Forest Lake does with its own sewer lines

The city's Sewer Department says it cleans its public sewer lines with high-pressure water jetting and vacuums debris at the manhole. It reports general jetting about once every five years, with trouble spots done annually or semi-annually, and it follows cleaning with a CCTV inspection to find defects.

That is the same logic a homeowner can borrow. A lateral that backs up repeatedly is a candidate for a jet pass followed by a camera look. The city also asks owners to notify it after cleaning a service line, because the process can push solids into the main.
