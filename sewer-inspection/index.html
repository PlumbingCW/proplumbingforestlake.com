Sewer Camera Inspection in Forest Lake, MN

A sewer camera inspection puts a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable and runs it through your sewer line, so you can see the pipe's actual condition on a monitor. It is the fastest way to answer whether a line is clogged, cracked, rooted, or collapsed.

This page covers inspections for Forest Lake home buyers, sellers, and owners dealing with slow drains or repeat backups, and what the findings mean.

What a sewer camera shows

The camera head carries lights and a lens, and a transmitter lets the plumber trace its position from the surface. As it moves, it reveals the pipe material, joints, scale, roots, standing water, and any break or offset. A recording lets you review the findings later.

Roots

Fine hair roots at a joint or a thick mass blocking the pipe.

Cracks and breaks

Longitudinal or circumferential cracks, or a section that has split.

Bellies

Low spots where water and solids collect and slow the flow.

Offset joints

Pipe sections that have shifted so that the opening is partly blocked.

When an inspection is worth scheduling

  • Before buying a home, especially an older one or one with large trees near the line.
  • After more than one backup, or after a clog that returned within months.
  • Before hydro jetting or lining, so the pipe condition is known.
  • When selling, to avoid a surprise during the buyer's inspection.

A camera look before hydro jetting protects the pipe, and a look before a repair tells the plumber what method fits. For an active backup, the work starts with emergency sewer line repair.

What the City of Forest Lake does and what you own

The City Sewer Department maintains the public sanitary sewer and uses CCTV inspections after cleaning to find defects in its own lines. It does not inspect the private service pipe from the main to your house. That portion belongs to the owner, and a private camera inspection is the way to see it.

Forest Lake is the first manhole in the Metropolitan Council sanitary sewer district, the northernmost community the council serves, and wastewater is treated in St. Paul. The practical point for owners is that the public side is a municipal system with its own maintenance schedule, and the private lateral is the part an inspection can reveal.

Septic systems are a different inspection

Homes in rural areas around Forest Lake may be on septic, not city sewer. In Washington County, Public Health and Environment regulates septic systems, and a compliance inspection is required when a property is sold unless one was filed in the last three years or a new system has a recent certificate. A septic inspection is done by a licensed septic inspector, not by a camera in a sewer lateral.

Safety steps before the plumber arrives

  • Keep children and pets away from any sewage on the floor.
  • Stop running water and flushing toilets while a backup is active.
  • Wash hands well after any contact with drain water.
  • Do not pour chemical drain openers into a backed up line.

What the city asks of residents

According to the City of Forest Lake, wipes, feminine hygiene products, paper towels, floss, and cat litter do not belong in the sewer. When a backup is reported, the city checks the public main first, and the pipe from the main to your house is the owner's to maintain.
