Basement Waterproofing in Forest Lake, MN

A basement that takes on water after rain or snowmelt is telling you where the water is coming from, and the fix depends on the entry point. Waterproofing is the work of keeping groundwater and surface water out, by managing it outside, collecting it inside, or both.

This page covers the plumbing-side solutions for Forest Lake homes: interior drain tile, sump systems, and sealing, and how to decide what your basement needs.

Before spending on waterproofing, rule out simple causes. Gutters and downspouts that dump next to the foundation, soil that slopes toward the house, and clogged window wells cause many wet basements. These are cheaper to fix than interior systems.

Interior drain tile and sump systems

An interior drain tile system is a perforated pipe laid along the footing inside the basement, under the slab edge. It collects water that rises at the floor-wall joint and carries it to a sump pit, where a pump sends it outside. It treats the water instead of trying to hold it back.

This is a plumbing-heavy approach, and the sump pump is the key part. Read the sump pump page for what a good pit, check valve, and discharge look like. The discharge must go outside or to a storm sewer, as Forest Lake's code does not allow it into the sanitary sewer.

Sealing and crack repair

Cracks in poured walls can be injected with polyurethane or epoxy from the inside to stop active leaks. This works well for isolated cracks, but it does not address water pressure around the whole foundation. If the water is coming from many places, drain tile is the more reliable system.

Interior drain tile is a good fit when

  • Water enters at the floor-wall joint
  • The basement is finished or will be
  • Exterior excavation is not practical

Exterior fixes are a better fit when

  • Gutters and grading are the cause
  • A single wall is failing
  • You can excavate and want to reduce water pressure on the wall

Water, lakes, and the Forest Lake area

Forest Lake is the largest lake in Washington County, covering about 2,220 acres, and more than 8,160 acres of land and water drain into it, according to the Comfort Lake-Forest Lake Watershed District. For homeowners that means a lot of surface water moves through the area toward the lake, and lakeside lots sit near that water.

Low ground, lakeside lots, and spring thaw all raise the odds of a wet basement. A plumber can check the pit and drainage, but grading and drainage design may also involve a landscaper or the watershed district, so call both if the problem is outside the house.
