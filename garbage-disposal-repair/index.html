Humming, jammed, or dead: what each symptom means

Garbage disposal symptoms

A jam is the easiest to fix. The plumber turns off power at the switch or the breaker, frees the flywheel from underneath, and clears whatever caused the jam. A leak from the body is not repairable, because the housing seals are inside the unit.

Never put your hand in the disposal

Even when it seems off, a disposal can start if the switch is flipped. Cut the power before any work, and use tongs or pliers to remove an object from the chamber. If the disposal is hard-wired, switch off the breaker.

What kills a disposal early

  • Grease and oil that coat the grinder and the drain line.
  • Fibrous waste such as celery, corn husks, and potato peels.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells that pack into the drain.
  • Running it without water, or running water for only a moment after.
  • Bones, fruit pits, and other hard items it was not built to grind.

Most disposal clogs are in the drain line below, not in the unit. A clogged line may need drain cleaning to restore flow, especially if the kitchen branch is shared with a dishwasher.

Installing a new disposal

A new disposal is sized by motor power. A half horsepower unit suits light use, and three quarter or one horsepower units handle more. Install involves removing the old unit, replacing the sink flange and mounting, connecting the dishwasher drain and the discharge line, and testing for leaks.

If your kitchen has no disposal today, adding one may require an outlet, a switch, and a change to the drain. These add to the job, and the plumber explains them before starting. For a broader kitchen update, see plumbing installation.

Disposals, septic tanks, and the sewer in the Forest Lake area

City of Forest Lake homes connected to the public sewer send disposal waste to the city system, which asks residents to keep grease and wipes out of the drain. Homes on septic systems are different, because food waste adds solids to the tank. If you are on septic, ask about septic-friendly use and pumping frequency, and know that Washington County Public Health and Environment regulates septic systems.

Safety steps before the plumber arrives

  • Never put your hand in the disposal, even when it is off.
  • Switch off the disposal and its breaker before you clear a jam.
  • Use tongs or pliers to remove anything stuck, not your fingers.
  • Do not pour chemical drain openers into a jammed or backed up sink.

What the city asks of residents

According to the City of Forest Lake, wipes, feminine hygiene products, paper towels, floss, and cat litter do not belong in the sewer. When a backup is reported, the city checks the public main first, and the pipe from the main to your house is the owner's to maintain.
