Whole-House Repiping in Forest Lake, MN

Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes throughout a house, usually because the old pipes are corroding, leaking, or restricting flow. It is a big job that is easier to plan than to rush, and it prevents repeated leak repairs on a failing system.

This page is for Forest Lake homeowners who are seeing the warning signs of failing pipes and want to know what repiping involves, what it costs to think about, and how to decide.

Signs your pipes need to be replaced

Repeated leaks

A second or third leak in the same system in a short span suggests the pipe is failing everywhere, not just at one spot.

Discolored water

Brown or rusty water from galvanized pipe means the inside is corroding.

Low pressure

Rust and scale narrow galvanized pipe until water barely flows.

Visible corrosion

Green on copper joints or flaking on steel pipe means the metal is wearing out.

A single leak does not mean repiping. A pinhole in one copper elbow is a repair. A string of leaks, or confirmed failing material, is the point to compare repair costs against replacement. Leak detection helps confirm whether the problem is local or systemic.

Pipe materials you may find in an older home

Supply pipe materials and what to know
MaterialTypical age or eraConcern
Galvanized steelCommon in older homesCorrodes from the inside, restricts flow, and leaks
PolybutyleneUsed in some homes from the late 1970s into the 1990sKnown for failing at fittings and flagged by insurers
CopperLong-lived, widely usedCan pit or develop pinholes in some water
PEXCommon in newer homes and replacementsFlexible, freeze tolerant, and quick to install

If you are not sure what you have, a plumber can identify the material at the water heater, under sinks, and in the basement. The material decides the urgency, because polybutylene and galvanized pipe tend to fail without much warning.

PEX or copper: which to choose

PEX tends to win on

  • Speed of installation, with fewer cuts into walls
  • Freeze tolerance, since it can expand a little
  • Lower cost on a whole-house job

Copper tends to win on

  • A long track record and a traditional feel
  • Heat tolerance in exposed runs
  • Preference of some buyers and insurers

Both are code-approved for supply plumbing. The plumber can explain the trade-offs for your layout, and which one fits a house with finished walls versus a house with open basement ceilings.

What to expect during a repipe

Plan the routes

The plumber maps new runs through basements, attics, and wall cavities to limit demolition.

Shut the water and open access

Small access holes are cut in walls and ceilings where lines must pass.

Install the new lines

New pipe is run to every fixture and tied into the main shutoff and the water heater.

Test and patch

The system is pressure tested, and access holes are patched. Painting is usually a separate step.

Most whole-house repipes take a few days, and the house may be without water for part of each day. Planning around a vacation or a remodel helps.

Forest Lake homes, ages, and repiping

The city annexed Forest Lake Township in 2001, and Census counts show the population growing from 6,856 in 2000 to 18,375 in 2010. Many homes in the area are therefore newer, which usually means copper or PEX. Older houses near downtown and lakeside cabins are the ones most likely to have galvanized or older copper lines.

If you buy an older house here, ask about the supply pipe material early. A galvanized system that looks fine today may need replacement soon, and the price is better known before closing. Leaks in older systems often start with a burst pipe on a cold night.
